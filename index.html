FOUNDATION

Understanding Pandu Roga

Ayurvedic concept

Pandu Roga is described as a disorder characterized prominently by pallor and altered complexion, with manifestations varying according to the predominance of Dosha. Charaka discusses its Nidana, Samprapti, Lakshana and Chikitsa in Chikitsa Sthana, chapter 16.

Modern note

Pandu is often discussed in relation to anemia in Ayurvedic teaching, but it should not be assumed that every case of pallor is anemia or that an Ayurvedic diagnosis automatically identifies the modern cause. Clinical assessment and appropriate investigations are important.

CLASSIFICATION

Five Types of Pandu

01

Vataja

Vata-dominant presentation with features described in the classical text.

02

Pittaja

Pitta-dominant presentation with characteristic changes in colour and heat-related features.

03

Kaphaja

Kapha-dominant presentation, including heaviness and other Kapha-associated features.

04

Sannipataja

Features associated with involvement of all three Doshas.

05

Mrid-bhakshana-janya

Pandu associated with habitual consumption of clay/earth as described classically.

03

CLINICAL FEATURES

Rupa & Lakshana

01Pallor / altered complexion

Change in normal colour and luster is a central feature.

02Aruchi & Daurbalya

Reduced appetite and weakness may be described.

03Shrama & Bhrama

Fatigue and giddiness are among the classical manifestations.

04Shwasa on exertion

Breathlessness, particularly with exertion, is described.

05Gaurava

A sense of heaviness may occur.

06Dosha-specific features

Specific features are interpreted according to Dosha dominance.

CORE TOPIC

Pandu Roga Chikitsa

Classical treatment framework

Charaka describes management after assessment of the patient and the condition. The chapter emphasizes Shodhana in appropriate, eligible cases, followed by suitable diet and Dosha-specific management. Treatment must be individualized according to Dosha, strength and clinical status.

मुख्य सिद्धान्तदोषबल • रोगबल • रोगिबलClinical judgement and individualized selection are essential.
🪔

Snehana

Internal oleation is described as part of the preparatory approach before Shodhana in suitable patients.

🌿

Shodhana

Charaka describes strong emetic and purgation therapies for appropriate Pandu cases after Snehana. These procedures require qualified supervision.

🍃

Shamana

Medicinal management is selected according to the predominant Dosha and the patient's strength and condition.

🥣

Brimhana & Pathya

After appropriate cleansing, wholesome, nourishing foods are described to support recovery and strength.

STUDENT NOTES

Important Treatment Points

① Assess before treating

Classical management is not a one-size-fits-all prescription. Patient strength, Dosha predominance and disease status influence the therapeutic choice.

② Panchakarma needs supervision

Vamana, Virechana and other Shodhana procedures should not be self-administered. They require proper assessment, preparation, procedure and follow-up.

③ Diet matters

Charaka describes old Shali rice, barley and wheat with suitable Yusha and Jangala Mamsa Rasa among Pathya foods after cleansing.

④ Modern evaluation

When anemia is suspected, investigations such as CBC and iron studies may be clinically relevant depending on the presentation. Identify and treat the underlying cause.

06

DIET & REGIMEN

Pathya – Apathya

✓ Pathya

  • Wholesome, easily digestible foods as appropriate
  • Old Shali rice, barley and wheat are classically mentioned
  • Suitable Mudga, Adhaki and Masura Yusha
  • Individualized diet according to Dosha and Agni

△ Apathya / Caution

  • Diet and habits that aggravate the involved Dosha
  • Heavy or incompatible foods when Agni is impaired
  • Self-medication with classical formulations
  • Delay in evaluation of significant pallor, weakness or breathlessness
